# The Intimate Table — The House

> You don't open an app.
> You come back to a place.

## What this is

The Intimate Table organises real-life experiences — dinners, conversations,
movement, food, travel, retreats — as **Chapters**.

**The House** is the digital place those Chapters leave things behind in. It is
not where the experience happens. It is where the experience is *kept*.

A person enters, walks from room to room, opens a drawer, finds an envelope
tied with black thread, a place card with a shell resting on it, a cassette with
a handwritten label. Each object holds something they actually lived.

The House starts almost empty. It fills slowly, only with what has happened.
Two people who enter it will not see the same house.

## The test we apply to every decision

1. *Does this make someone feel they are visiting a place, or using an app?*
   If "using an app", redesign it.
2. *Would this still matter if there were no engagement metrics?*
   If not, it does not belong.

The goal is for someone to think: **"I want to go back there, because something
I lived is there."** Not: "I should check what's new."

## Principles

| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Real life first | No streaks, no reminders to "come back". During a Chapter the phone mostly disappears. |
| Memory over content | Nothing is a post. Everything is an object with a memory attached. There is no feed. |
| Discovery over navigation | You move through doorways, not tabs. The plan of the house is a drawing, not a menu. |
| Emotion over gamification | No points, levels, badges, follower counts, or progress bars. |
| Invisible technology | A rule engine decides what appears and when. The interface only ever shows paper, ink, light. |
| Not everything is explained | Some objects are sealed. Some doors aren't there yet. A clue can be just a sentence. |
| Personal, not social | People you met appear as place cards. You can find each other again only if both of you want to. |

## The rooms

- **The Hall** — the entrance, and *now*. A typewritten letter from The House, an
  envelope for the next Chapter, something that recently appeared, a door that
  wasn't there before. No "Upcoming events", no "Notifications".
- **The Table** — the emotional heart. Place cards of people met, menus, what was
  said, recipes. Reconnection requires mutual consent. No following.
- **The Library** — curiosity. Books, notebooks, cards, letters from the Chapters.
  Some only appear on the shelf because you were at a particular table.
- **The Studio** — the body. Not tracking. A pair of shoes from a morning run opens
  the route, the coach, the song, what was learned.
- **The Memory Room** — the most important room. Dark, lit only on the objects.
  Almost empty at first. After years, the reason to come back.
- **The Unmarked Door** — sometimes there, often not. Appears because of a
  Chapter, an anniversary, a private invitation, a season.

## The life of a Chapter

```
   PRELUDE                    DURING                     AFTERGLOW
   ───────                    ──────                     ─────────
   one envelope in the Hall   the House goes quiet       the next morning,
   one question / clue /      "the table is set —        the House has changed:
   dress cue / thing to bring  put the phone away"       new objects, a recipe,
                              one reveal (a card, a QR)  a photograph, a fragment
```

One meaningful interaction is better than ten notifications.

## Chapter 0 — Thirty

The opening of The House. Not a birthday: a threshold. Doing things before
feeling ready. Participation over preparation.

> *What would you do if being good at it wasn't a requirement?*

Before Chapter 0 the House holds almost nothing — an envelope and that question.
After Chapter 0 the Memory Room has its first objects.

## Visual language (from the moodboard)

- **Paper and thread.** Cream stock, black cotton thread wrapped around envelopes.
  Sealed things are *tied*, not padlocked.
- **Spaced capitals.** Small, widely-tracked serif capitals, like `LES THÈMES`
  printed on an envelope flap or a name on a place card.
- **Objects resting on cards.** A shell on a place card: the person and the thing
  that marks them.
- **The typewriter.** The House writes to you in typewritten letters. Imperfect,
  warm, human.
- **The cassette.** Music as an object with a hand-written label, sides A and B.
- **Light, not colour.** Each room is defined by its light: the Hall by evening
  cream, the Table by dark wood, the Library by green-black ink, the Studio by
  morning limestone, the Memory Room by near-darkness with objects lit.

### Second moodboard: the house itself

- **Plaster and arches.** Warm sand plaster, horseshoe arches, doorways that
  show the light of the next room.
- **Light through leaves.** Late sun cut by palm fronds on walls and floors;
  shadows that move a little.
- **Zellige, terracotta, brass, burgundy linen.** A tea tray left on green
  tiles; a napkin dropped on the floor.
- **The sea at the end of a corridor.** Evening colour, never a postcard.

### Human traces

Every room answers: *where are the traces?* A glass not quite finished, a
napkin dropped, candles burned down, a book left face down, a coffee gone cold,
a towel after the run. Never staged people looking at the camera.

We do not want it to look like Airbnb, ClassPass, Headspace, Notion, a SaaS
dashboard, an events marketplace or a generic luxury hotel.
